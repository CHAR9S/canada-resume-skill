# Canadian resume conventions

These are defaults for ordinary job applications, not universal rules. The employer's explicit application instructions take precedence. See [sources](sources.md) for provenance.

## Contact and personal details

Use name, contact information, and relevant professional links. City/province is a practical project default; do not manufacture a Canadian address for a person abroad. Include relocation intent only when confirmed. Do not request a full street address just to perform a review.

Omit photographs and sensitive identifiers such as SIN or passport numbers from ordinary resume drafts. Birthdate and marital status do not belong in a standard application resume. Do not repeat removed sensitive details in the change log. A separate portfolio or role-specific casting brief is a different deliverable.

References are normally provided separately if requested. Never invent referees or publish their contact details. Citizenship, immigration status, and eligibility claims must not be inferred from a name, address, education, or work history.

## Length and organization

Start with one to two pages for a conventional resume, but do not remove essential evidence to meet an arbitrary page count. Academic CVs and application systems with detailed screening instructions need their own approach.

Recent relevant experience usually deserves more space. The source handout's 10–15-year window is a prioritization heuristic, not an instruction to erase older relevant achievements or alter dates. Condense earlier roles when appropriate and preserve chronology.

Use clear headings such as Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Certifications, Projects, or Volunteer Experience. Include only useful sections. Separate paid employment from projects and volunteering. For early-career applicants, relevant education or projects can precede work history.

## Readability and parsing

Project implementation default: a simple single-column layout, selectable text, consistent bullets, and ordinary section titles. Avoid critical information only in icons, images, headers, footers, or decorative text boxes. This reduces avoidable reading-order problems; it is not a guarantee about every employer's software.

Start around 11–12 pt body text with comfortable spacing. Use a legible common font; the handout's Arial and Times New Roman are examples, not the only choices. Keep presentation consistent with any companion documents requested.

Use the employer's requested file type. If no type is specified, choose a readable document format appropriate to the workflow and test the actual export. Do not claim a Markdown draft has a verified page count.

## Language and context

Prefer clear Canadian English for English applications; preserve proper names, official titles, and product spellings. Follow the posting and user's preference for French or bilingual applications. Translation does not establish language proficiency.

Keep foreign education and international experience visible when relevant. Explain transferable work rather than treating a lack of Canadian employment as disqualification. Licences, professional titles, credential assessments, and province-specific requirements must be stated exactly as verified; check the relevant regulator when a current eligibility question arises.
